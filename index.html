MY Denver Card Wallet

One wallet for out-of-school funds and the childcare subsidy. Families can direct a payment to an approved provider and attach who it's for and what it covers, so the provider can match it to a sign-up without a phone call. Click through the phone; the provider's ledger on the right updates as you go.

For discussion

What this version adds

  • Wallet is the center of the app. It's the middle nav button and shows one total across funding pockets.
  • Two pockets with their own rules. Out-of-school funds (My Spark) and the childcare subsidy (CCCAP) each limit which kids and which approved providers they can pay.
  • Pay a provider from the app. Pick the pocket and an approved provider, then say which child and which sign-up or care period the payment covers. The balance drops right away.
  • The card still works at the counter. It's restricted to approved providers, and each swipe asks the family to add details afterward.

Making reconciliation easier

  • In prototypeStructured payment details, not just a memo. Each payment carries the child, the enrollment ID or care period, and the provider's account number. A free-text note is optional.
  • In prototypeA reference code on every payment, shown to both the family and the provider.
  • In prototypeProvider payment requests. The provider sends a request tied to an enrollment or invoice, and the family approves it. This is the closest we get to joining registration and payment without building registration for every provider.
  • In prototypeAdd details after a card swipe, so tap-to-pay purchases don't end up as unmatched deposits.
  • To discussPay at checkout when registering in the app. This reuses the existing funding waterfall, so the payment is never separate from the sign-up.
  • To discussA provider export or integration: a downloadable statement first, then a feed into the registration and billing tools providers already use.

Questions to settle

  • How does CCCAP actually move? Today the subsidy pays providers directly based on attendance. Would a wallet sit alongside that or replace it? This needs early input from the state and county administrators.
  • Who approves providers for each pocket, and how often does that list sync?
  • How much child information should providers see? The prototype shows first name and last initial.
  • Parent fees and overages: how does the family pay what the pocket doesn't cover?
  • Timing of provider requests: what happens to a request the family never approves, and how do refunds return to a pocket?
